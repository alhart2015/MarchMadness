## Diagnosis

The pattern from the Massey-decay-14d experiment repeated almost exactly:

| experiment | clause 1 corr vs adj_em | clause 2 LL delta |
|------------|--------------------------|-------------------|
| Massey-decay (hl=14d) | 0.931 (PASS, mean) | +0.0057 (FAIL) |
| Colley                | 0.907 (PASS, mean) | +0.0053 (FAIL) |
| Massey-uniform        | 0.957 (FAIL) | not run (clause 1 short-circuit) |

Two structurally distinct rating systems (margin-decayed Massey vs
W/L-only-with-prior Colley) both passed clause 1 by clear margins
against `adj_em`, and both failed clause 2 by roughly the same delta.
This is strong evidence that the failure is not about *which*
ranking algorithm we use -- it's about whether ANY single new
opponent-adjusted-strength feature can carry information the v4
67-feature stack doesn't already extract.

At v4's data scale (~2898 tourney games for training, double-LOSO
under v9-C is even thinner), individual feature distinctness is
necessary but not sufficient. The marginal information content of
each additional rating feature -- even one that's only ~0.69
correlated with the most-similar existing feature -- gets lost in
the noise floor of XGBoost trained on a finite sample.

The 2022 outlier (delta +0.0135) is consistent with this: 2022 was a
chaos tournament with multiple double-digit upsets; a noisy new
feature is more likely to push the model toward bad confidence
adjustments in such years. The 2024 marginal help is plausibly
the "different opponent adjustment" angle paying off in a more
chalky year, but +0.005 isn't enough to compensate for the average
hurt across years.

## Implications for queue items #2-#4

The structural lesson now generalizes:

1. **TODO #2 (hierarchical BT with feature priors) is the next-best
   angle.** Rather than adding a parallel feature, this couples a
   ratings model TO v4 features through Bayesian priors:
   `s_team ~ Normal(beta . v4_features_team, sigma)`. The ratings
   model gains standalone strength from the prior; the prior gains
   ratings-model insight from the data. The structural risk is
   different (residual correlation regressing toward 0.77 as the
   models re-converge -- the LR-stage1 failure mode), but the gating
   pattern from Massey/Colley is reusable.
2. **TODO #3 (small MLP as stage-1) faces the same correlated-error
   risk if it reuses the v4 67-feature matrix.** The Colley result
   strengthens the case that just adding capacity to the same
   feature matrix won't help.
3. **TODO #5 (external rankings: 538 / KenPom-public / BPI) is now
   higher-priority than TODO #4 (full Bayesian BT).** External
   composites that mix in different signals (especially anything
   non-public-data, like roster-injury info or proprietary models)
   are the most likely path to information v4 doesn't have.

## Branch summary (Massey + Colley closed together)

This branch was set up to test both Massey and Colley as v4
features under TODO #1. Net result:

- Massey-MOV (uniform): clause 1 FAIL (corr 0.957 vs adj_em).
- Massey-MOV (decay sweep, hl=7..120d): clause 1 passed at hl in
  {7d, 14d}, but hl=14d failed clause 2 (+0.0057 LL).
- Colley: clause 1 PASS (3 baselines), clause 2 FAIL (+0.0053 LL).

All three failure modes share a common root: v4's 67-feature stack
already extracts the "team strength" signal in enough independent
ways that adding a single more rating feature provides no
marginal value. Future ratings-model work should pivot to the
prior-couples-to-features architecture (TODO #2), not parallel
features.
